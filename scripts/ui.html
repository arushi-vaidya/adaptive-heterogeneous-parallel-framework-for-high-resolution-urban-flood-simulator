Problem Statement

High-resolution urban flood simulation is computationally expensive due to large spatial domains, fine resolution and repeated time-step calculations of the shallow-water equations.

Large
Spatial Domains

Cities require millions of grid cells.

Repeated
Time Steps

Thousands of iterations for accurate results.

Complex
Physics

Shallow-water equations with terrain, drainage and boundary conditions.

High
Computational Cost

Results in long simulation time and large memory requirements.

Literature
Survey

Recent studies have advanced GPU-accelerated and parallel computing approaches for shallow-water based flood simulation, focusing on adaptive resolution, multi-GPU execution and large-scale urban flood modelling.

Paper (IEEE Format)
Key Contribution & Relevance
01S. Saleem and M. R. Norman, “A scalable shallow-water solver for flood modelling using MPI, OpenACC and GPUs,” Environmental Modelling & Software, vol. 181, Art. no. 106364, 2024.
Developed a parallel GPU-accelerated shallow-water solver using MPI and OpenACC, achieving significant speedup and strong scalability for large-scale flood simulations.
02Y. Rong, X. Liu, and Z. Wang, “GPU-accelerated urban flood modelling with nonuniform grids,” Journal of Hydrology, vol. 643, Art. no. 131972, 2024.
Proposed a GPU-based flood model that employs nonuniform/adaptive grids to capture urban features (e.g., buildings, roads) efficiently while maintaining accuracy.
03X. Wang, H. Li, and Q. Zhang, “A GPU-accelerated shallow-water model with local time stepping for efficient flood simulation,” Advances in Water Resources, vol. 186, Art. no. 104724, 2024.
Introduced a local time-stepping strategy on GPUs to improve computational efficiency for shallow-water simulations, especially for domains with varying flow conditions.
04J. Chen, Z. Li, and Y. Zhao, “Multi-GPU urban flood simulation with adaptive mesh refinement and load balancing,” Computers & Fluids, vol. 281, Art. no. 106354, 2025.
Presented a multi-GPU framework with adaptive mesh refinement (AMR) and dynamic load balancing, reducing runtime while preserving flood prediction accuracy.
05W. Dong, L. Guo, and H. Sun, “A multi-GPU finite-volume shallow-water model for large-scale flood simulation,” Water Resources Research, vol. 61, no. 4, Art. no. e2024WR036821, 2025.
Developed an efficient multi-GPU finite-volume solver for shallow-water equations, enabling high-resolution and large-domain urban flood simulations with strong parallel scalability.
06A. Chowdhury and G. Kesserwani, “LISFLOOD-FP 8.2: GPU-accelerated flood modelling with on-the-fly dynamic resolution adaptivity,” Environmental Modelling & Software, vol. 188, Art. no. 106488, 2025.
Extended LISFLOOD-FP with GPU acceleration and dynamic resolution adaptivity, demonstrating faster simulations and improved efficiency for high-resolution urban flood scenarios.

Objectives

01

Develop a high-resolution urban flood simulator using heterogeneous parallel computing.

02

Implement and optimize a parallel solver using CUDA and OpenMP for efficient execution.

03

Introduce adaptive spatial resolution based on flood activity to improve accuracy and computational efficiency.

04

Evaluate runtime, speedup, scalability and accuracy to analyse the effectiveness of the proposed approach.

Novelty & Contribution

Addressing the gap in efficient and scalable high-resolution urban flood simulation.

Existing Work

GPU accelerationSignificant speedups for shallow-water simulations.
Multi-GPU / Distributed ExecutionDomain decomposition using MPI and OpenACC.
Optimized Numerical SolversEfficient schemes for partially dry domains and large-scale simulations.
High-Resolution ModellingDemonstrated feasibility of large-scale urban flood simulations.

Limitations / Open Challenges

Uniform computational effortSame resolution across the entire domain, even in low-activity regions.
Resource inefficiencyHigh computational and memory cost for large domains.
Limited adaptive strategiesAdaptive resolution and dynamic load balancing are still not widely integrated in heterogeneous setups.
Scalability concernsCommunication overhead and efficient workload distribution remain challenging for large, high-resolution simulations.

Proposed Novelty

01
Adaptive Spatial ResolutionDynamically refine computational regions based on flood activity to reduce unnecessary computation while preserving accuracy.
02
Heterogeneous Parallel FrameworkIntegrated implementation using OpenMP, CUDA, MPI and OpenACC for efficient CPU–GPU and distributed execution.
03
Comprehensive EvaluationSystematic analysis of runtime, speedup, scalability, memory usage and accuracy trade-offs for adaptive high-resolution urban flood simulation.
